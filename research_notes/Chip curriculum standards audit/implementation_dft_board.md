# VLSI Implementation (STA, circuit design, layout/PD, low power), Design for Test, and Board SI/PI: standards and TAPEOUT content audit (as of October 2026)

Scope audited (read in full or skimmed for claims): src/lessons/vlsi.ts (sta, vlsi, power, pcb), src/lessons/verification.ts (dft lesson, lines 157-191), src/lessons/roles.ts (sipi 150-190, pnr 194-226, ate 375-400), src/lessons/frontier.ts (libchar 44-72; emerging 280-306 for node currency), src/practice/vlsi.ts (all), src/practice/verification.ts (dftPractice 402-475, boss-dft 635-651, boss-upf 653-671), src/practice/silicon.ts (pnrPractice 19-133, boss-pd 135-151, atePractice 623-681, boss-test 683-698), src/games/timing/*, src/games/sta2/levels.ts, src/games/layout/*, src/games/pd/*, src/games/dft/*, src/games/pcb/*, src/games/circuit/levels.ts (clab-pdn 443-464), src/glossary.ts. No repo files were modified.

Overall verdict: the implementation/DFT/board content is unusually accurate for an educational game. The core equations (setup/hold slack, multicycle -setup N / -hold N-1, CRPR credit = common delay x (late - early), Elmore, logical effort g and p values, alpha*C*V^2*f, Williams-Brown DL, March C- 10N, MATS 4N, JTAG 16-state TAP and 5x TMS=1 reset, scan test time, target impedance, SRF, L*di/dt) are all correct and internally consistent. I found no severity-5 errors. The real weaknesses are (a) missing industry vocabulary that interviewers expect (LVF/POCV, MMMC/RC corners, test coverage vs fault coverage, IEEE 1500/1687/1838, UPF version, LEF/DEF/SPEF), (b) a handful of imprecise or inconsistent terms (CRPR expansion, alpha definition wording, corner temperatures in boss-sta), and (c) process-node content that frames backside power as "next" when it is in high-volume production in 2026 and is absent from the PD lessons.

Severity scale used: 5 = teaches something wrong that would hurt in an interview/job; 4 = materially wrong or misleading; 3 = important gap or imprecision a hiring panel would probe; 2 = minor imprecision/inconsistency; 1 = cosmetic or nit.

## 1. Governing standards and their current versions (Oct 2026), and whether the game names them

### Takeaway
The current baselines are IEEE 1801-2024 (UPF 4.0), Liberty with LVF for variation, SDC 2.1, IEEE 1149.1-2013, IEEE 1500-2022, IEEE 1687-2014, IEEE 1838-2019, STDF V4-2007, IPC-2152 (current capacity, supersedes IPC-2221 charts) and IPC-7352 (supersedes IPC-7351). The game names almost none of these by number: only "IEEE 1149.1" (JTAG), "UPF" (no version), "IPC-2141" (microstrip formula), "LEF" once and "GDSII/OASIS" in the glossary.

### Cited Findings
- IEEE 1801-2024 (UPF 4.0) was published by IEEE in early 2025 (March 4) and Accellera announced on March 19, 2025 that it is free through the IEEE GET program; it adds virtual supply nets, refinable macros, UPF libraries, value conversion methods (VCM) and HDL tunneling for AMS, and reworked state-retention modeling — [EEJournal/Accellera announcement](https://eejournal.com/industry_news/accellera-announces-ieee-standard-1801-2024-is-available-through-ieee-get-program); [SemiEngineering: An inside look at UPF 4.0](https://semiengineering.com/an-inside-look-at-upf-4-0); [DVCon paper on IEEE 1801-2024](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/Introduction-of-IEEE-1801-2024-UPF4.0-improvements-for-the-specification-and-verification-of-low-power.pdf). This supersedes IEEE 1801-2018 (UPF 3.1).
  - Game: UPF is mentioned without a version or IEEE number (src/lessons/vlsi.ts:153 "The power intent is described in UPF."; src/practice/vlsi.ts:239; src/glossary.ts:79). Severity 2 gap: add "IEEE 1801 (UPF); current revision 1801-2024, UPF 4.0".
- Liberty Variation Format (LVF) consolidated AOCV, POCV and SOCV modeling into one Liberty extension; LVF stores slew/load-dependent sigma per arc for delay, transition and constraints, and LVF .libs are described as mandatory for signoff at advanced nodes, where OCV can move timing/power by 50-100% at 7 nm — [SemiEngineering: Wrestling with variation](https://semiengineering.com/wrestling-with-variation-in-advanced-node-designs); [Synopsys Liberty additions (Design&Reuse)](https://design-reuse.com/news/35553/synopsys-liberty-library-format.html); [Synopsys SiliconSmart datasheet](https://origin-www.synopsys.com/content/dam/synopsys/implementation&signoff/datasheets/siliconsmart-ds.pdf).
  - Game: libchar covers NLDM and CCS only (src/lessons/frontier.ts:50, :69; recap :71 "CCS adds waveform accuracy."). No LVF, ECSM, AOCV or POCV anywhere (grep of src/ found zero hits). Severity 3 gap for the libchar and STA roles.
- SDC 2.1 is the current published version of the Synopsys Design Constraints specification (distributed via Synopsys TAP-in) — [ASIC notes: SDC](https://akiitr.is-a.dev/asic/asic/SDC); [Intel/Altera Quartus SDC definition](https://quartushelp.altera.com/current/reference/glossary/def_sdc.htm). Game uses correct SDC syntax (src/lessons/vlsi.ts:48-52) but does not name a version (fine).
- IEEE 1149.1-2013 is the current major revision of JTAG (revision of 1149.1-2001, more than doubled in size; adds PDL procedural language, excludable/selectable TDR segments, component initialization) — [Electronic Design: "This isn't your father's JTAG anymore"](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/test-measurement/article/21800579/ieee-std-11491-this-isnt-your-fathers-jtag-anymore-part-1). Game: "JTAG (IEEE 1149.1): a 4-5 pin port (TCK, TMS, TDI, TDO, optional TRST)" (src/lessons/verification.ts:183) is correct.
- IEEE 1500-2022 (embedded core test wrapper, with CTL) is the Active standard, board-approved 2022-06-16, published 2022-10-12, superseding 1500-2005 — [IEEE SA 1500-2022](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/1500/7704); [IEEE 1500-2005 inactive-reserved](https://standards.ieee.org/standard/1500-2005.html).
- IEEE 1687-2014 (IJTAG) is the instrument-access standard building on 1149.1 and 1500; IEEE 1838-2019 is the die-centric test-access standard for 3D stacked ICs reusing 1500/1149.1/1687 — [ASSET InterTech: How 3D stacks are being tested](https://asset-intertech.com/?p=2783); [3D InCites on P1838](https://www.3dincites.com/2015/01/defining-test-access-stacked-die/); [Nimonik IEEE 1838 listing](https://www.standards.nimonik.com/products/ieee/ieee-1838/).
  - Game: none of 1500 / 1687 / 1838 / 1149.6 / 1149.10 / STIL appear (grep: zero hits). Severity 3 gap: IJTAG and 1500 wrappers are everyday DFT-engineer vocabulary, and chiplet test (1838) is central to 2026 products.
- STDF V4 (2007 spec), originally Teradyne's, is the de facto industry format for ATE test results — [Wikipedia: Standard Test Data Format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Test_Data_Format); [SemiEngineering: Introduction to test data formats](https://semiengineering.com/introduction-to-test-data-formats). Game ate lesson/practice never mention STDF (gap, severity 2).
- IPC-2152 (2009) replaced the conductor-sizing charts of IPC-2221 (based on >50-year-old data) and accounts for planes, board thickness, vias and material — [Cadence: IPC-2152 vs IPC-2221](https://resources.system-analysis.cadence.com/blog/ipc-2152-vs-ipc-2221-which-to-use-for-pcb-thermal-analysis); [Circuits Assembly: IPC releases IPC-2152](https://circuitsassembly.com/ca/editorial/menu-new-indust-prod/17617-ipc-releases-ipc-2152-standard.html). Game has no current-capacity content (gap, severity 1-2).
- IPC-7352 completely replaces IPC-7351 (adds through-hole, expands naming convention) — [PCB Libraries forum thread](https://www.pcblibraries.com/forum/topic3150&OB=DESC.html) (forum source; treat as indicative). Game has no land-pattern content (no claim to check).
- IPC-6012 defines Class 1/2/3 performance classes; one vendor blog states IPC-6012F was released in Q1 2026 — [AtlasPCB news](https://atlaspcb.com/news/news-ipc-6012f-automotive-pcb-reliability-2026); [Sierra Circuits: IPC Class 3](https://sierraconnect.protoexpress.com/t/pcb-glossary-ipc-class-3/3850). Low-quality source for the release date; see Gaps. Game does not mention IPC classes.
- IPC-2141 microstrip formula: the game cites it correctly by name (src/practice/vlsi.ts:253) — see section 6 for a validity-range problem.
- Tool vendor landscape changed: Synopsys completed its acquisition of Ansys on July 17, 2025 — [Synopsys 10-Q FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/883241/000088324125000024/snps-20250731.htm); [Photoniques: Synopsys completes acquisition of Ansys](https://www.photoniques.com/en/news/5305-synopsys-completes-acquisition-of-ansys).
  - Game src/lessons/roles.ts:155: "Tools include Ansys (SIwave, HFSS, RedHawk), Cadence Sigrity, Keysight ADS and Voltus." Two issues: Ansys tools are now Synopsys products, and "Voltus" (a Cadence product) reads as if it were Keysight's. Suggested: "Synopsys (formerly Ansys: SIwave, HFSS, RedHawk-SC), Cadence (Sigrity, Clarity, Voltus) and Keysight ADS." Severity 1.

### Inferences
- A one-screen "standards you will hear on the job" card per act (UPF 1801-2024, Liberty/LVF, SDC 2.1, SPEF, LEF/DEF, GDSII/OASIS, 1149.1/1500/1687/1838, STIL/STDF, IPC-2152/2141/6012/7352) would close most standards gaps cheaply without changing gameplay.
- Because the game rarely cites version numbers, almost nothing in it is "outdated" by version; the risk is omission, not staleness.

### Gaps
- Could not verify in this session (from prior knowledge only, so not stated as findings): IEEE 1481-2019 as the current delay/power calculation + SPEF standard; LEF/DEF 5.8 as current Cadence release; OASIS as SEMI P39 and OASIS.MASK P44; IEEE 1450 STIL family (1450-1999, 1450.1 etc.) status; IEEE 1149.6-2015 and 1149.10-2017; whether a P1149.1 revision is active in 2026; the current Liberty user-guide release number (Synopsys publishes Liberty yearly via TAP-in/Open Liberty).
- IPC-6012F release date: only a vendor blog asserts Q1 2026; IPC's own page was not checked.
- IPC-2141 current revision (2141A vs a later "B") not verified.
- Whether STDF ownership has moved to a standards body (a SEMI link appeared in search but was not examined).

## 2. Static timing analysis correctness

### Takeaway
STA equations, slack definitions, skew sign convention, WNS/TNS, multicycle setup/hold pairing and CRPR arithmetic are correct throughout. Weak points: the CRPR acronym is expanded inconsistently, the boss's corner temperatures contradict the game's own temperature-inversion note, RC corners/MMMC/AOCV/POCV are absent, and the STA2 report lab's hold model is a non-standard convention that should be labeled as such.

### Cited Findings
Correct content (verified against sources):
- Multicycle: "Remember to add `-hold ${n - 1}`, or the hold check moves too" (src/lessons/vlsi.ts:57); SDC example `set_multicycle_path 2 -setup` / `set_multicycle_path 1 -hold` (vlsi.ts:51-52); "By default the hold check follows the setup edge minus one cycle. Always pair -setup N with -hold N-1" (src/practice/vlsi.ts:44); STA2 lab brief (src/games/sta2/levels.ts:122). All match vendor documentation: if the setup multiplier moves the check N cycles, the hold check moves N-1 cycles, and a -hold multiplier restores it — [AMD UG903, "Setup=5 / Hold moved accordingly"](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug903-vivado-using-constraints/Example-One-Setup-5/Hold-Moved-Accordingly); [AMD UG835 set_multicycle_path](https://docs.amd.com/r/2024.1-English/ug835-vivado-tcl-commands/set_multicycle_path).
- Setup slack = T + skew - uncertainty - setup - (clk-Q + logic); hold slack = clk-Q + logic_min - (hold + skew [+ hold uncertainty]); positive skew helps setup and hurts hold; period absent from hold (vlsi.ts:19-22, :40, :61; practice/vlsi.ts:16-18, :27-29; timing/levels.ts:15-17, :72-73; timing/sta.ts:176-177). Correct and consistent.
- Temperature inversion is mentioned correctly in two places: "Slow process, low voltage (often high temperature, or low temperature in advanced nodes with temperature inversion)" (src/practice/vlsi.ts:80) and frontier.ts:68. In sub-100 nm and FinFET processes cell delay can decrease with rising temperature, so worst corners are no longer automatically at the traditional temperature extremes — [Lin et al., FinFET temperature effect inversion, ISLPED'14 (USC)](https://coldflux.usc.edu/~massoud/Papers/DTM-FinFET-TempEffeInv-islped14.pdf); [US patent 8225257 on temperature inversion](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8225257).

Findings (errors, imprecision, inconsistencies):
- F-STA-1 (severity 2, terminology). src/practice/vlsi.ts:59-60: "How much pessimism does CRPR (common path pessimism removal) give back" and walkthrough title "'CRPR (common path pessimism removal)'". CRPR stands for clock reconvergence pessimism removal (Synopsys PrimeTime term); common path pessimism removal is CPPR (Cadence Tempus/Innovus term). The STA2 lab correctly says "CRPR (clock reconvergence pessimism removal)" (src/games/sta2/levels.ts:89), so the game is internally inconsistent. Sources: [ChipEdge: What is CRPR in VLSI](https://chipedge.com/resources/?p=20005) (both names, often used interchangeably, tool-specific). Suggested correction: "CRPR/CPPR (clock reconvergence pessimism removal, called common path pessimism removal in Cadence tools)".
- F-STA-2 (severity 2, internal contradiction / outdated corner model). src/practice/vlsi.ts:324: boss art "Corner SS 0.72V 125C (setup)          Corner FF 0.88V -40C (hold)" and recap :336 "setup in SS, hold in FF". With temperature inversion (which the game itself teaches at :80), the slowest low-voltage corner can be at -40 C and the fastest corner can be at 125 C, so signoff checks setup and hold at both temperature extremes — [ISLPED'14 TEI paper](https://coldflux.usc.edu/~massoud/Papers/DTM-FinFET-TempEffeInv-islped14.pdf). Also src/practice/vlsi.ts:81 hold-corner answer "Fast process, high voltage: data paths are shortest" omits temperature. Acceptable as a simplification for a planar node, but the boss should either add "(planar node)" or list e.g. "SS 0.72V -40C/125C (setup), FF 0.88V -40C/125C (hold)".
- F-STA-3 (severity 2, non-standard convention presented as method). src/games/sta2/levels.ts:24 and brief :56: hold slack = "(launch latency + (clk-to-Q + data delays) x 0.7) - (capture latency + hold time + half the uncertainty)". Two simplifications: (a) clock latencies are not scaled to the fast corner while data delays are, whereas a real fast-corner run recomputes clock-network delays too; (b) "half the uncertainty" is an arbitrary in-game rule — real flows set hold uncertainty separately with `set_clock_uncertainty -hold` (SDC 2.1 command; [SDC notes](https://akiitr.is-a.dev/asic/asic/SDC)). Suggested: say "in this lab we use hold uncertainty = 50% of setup uncertainty; real projects specify -setup and -hold uncertainty separately" and either scale the clock latencies too or state that they are fixed for simplicity.
- F-STA-4 (severity 3, gap). No coverage of RC extraction corners (Cworst/Cbest/RCworst/RCbest), MMMC (multi-mode multi-corner) signoff, AOCV/POCV/SOCV or LVF; corners are presented only as PVT (src/lessons/vlsi.ts:45; glossary.ts:71 "Process-voltage-temperature corners; OCV derates variation within one die."). Industry signoff at advanced nodes uses POCV with LVF libraries — [SemiEngineering: Wrestling with variation](https://semiengineering.com/wrestling-with-variation-in-advanced-node-designs); [Cadence white paper on reducing timing pessimism at 16nm and below](https://newstaging.cadence.com/zh_TW/home/resources/white-papers/addressing-process-variation-and-reducing-timing-pessimism-at-16nm-and-below-wp.html). Suggest one step after vlsi.ts:45: "Flat OCV derates were replaced by depth/distance-based AOCV and then statistical POCV using per-arc sigma from LVF libraries; corners also include RC extraction corners, and all mode x corner combinations are signed off together (MMMC)."
- F-STA-5 (severity 1). src/lessons/vlsi.ts:20 hold equation `t_launch + t_cq + t_logic,min ≥ t_capture + t_hold` omits hold uncertainty while the setup line includes it; the practice bank (practice/vlsi.ts:27) includes "hold uncertainty". Acceptable simplification; consider adding "+ uncertainty_hold" for symmetry.
- F-STA-6 (severity 1). src/games/timing/levels.ts:73: "you can't fix hold by slowing the clock, which is why hold violations found after tapeout are fatal." Broadly true and good teaching; strictly, post-silicon hold failures are sometimes worked around by voltage/temperature or fixed with a metal ECO respin, so "very expensive (a respin)" is more precise than "fatal".
- F-STA-7 (severity 1, gap). set_clock_groups -asynchronous (practice/vlsi.ts:84) is correct; the lesson could note it differs from set_false_path in that tools still compute crosstalk between asynchronous groups (unverified in this session; not cited, so listed as suggestion only).

### Inferences
- The STA content would pass a junior PD/STA interview on mechanics. A senior interviewer would probe POCV/LVF, MMMC and RC corners, which the game does not teach (F-STA-4); this is the most valuable STA addition.
- Unifying the CRPR naming (F-STA-1) is a two-line fix with clear interview value, since both CRPR and CPPR are used in job postings.

### Gaps
- Bhasker & Chadha's textbook and the PrimeTime/Tempus user guides were not fetched; derate sign conventions (data path late derate on capture? clock-to-Q early derate for hold) were checked only against general sources. The game's convention (launch clock + data late, capture clock early for setup) is the standard one.

## 3. Low-power design (dynamic/leakage power, gating, multi-voltage, UPF, IR/EM)

### Takeaway
The power content is correct and consistent: the game uses P = alpha*C*V^2*f (no 1/2) everywhere, which matches Weste & Harris, and the clock gating, isolation, level-shifter, retention, in-rush and DVFS explanations are sound. Two wording issues (alpha definition, power-down order) and missing UPF version/2024 features are the only findings.

### Cited Findings
- In Weste & Harris's convention P_dynamic = alpha*C*VDD^2*f, a clock has alpha = 1 and a signal that switches once per cycle has alpha = 1/2 (alpha counts charging events, i.e., 0-to-1 transitions) — [Harris, E158 Lecture 7: Power](https://pages.hmc.edu/harris/class/e158/lect7-power.pdf); [HMC lecture 18](https://pages.hmc.edu/harris/class/e158/04/lect18.pdf).
  - Game is consistent: formula without 1/2 in src/lessons/vlsi.ts:132, src/practice/vlsi.ts:174-176, glossary.ts:76. No mixed use of 1/2*C*V^2*f was found in the audited files.
  - F-PWR-1 (severity 2, imprecise definition). src/lessons/vlsi.ts:133 "α activity factor (fraction of the capacitance switching per cycle)" and src/practice/vlsi.ts:175 "α (activity) is the fraction of the capacitance that actually switches each cycle". Under the no-1/2 formula, alpha must be the probability of a 0-to-1 (charging) transition per cycle; a node that toggles every cycle has alpha = 1/2, the clock alpha = 1 ([Harris lect7](https://pages.hmc.edu/harris/class/e158/lect7-power.pdf)). A learner who reads "switching" as "toggling" will overestimate power by 2x, and interviewers often use the 1/2*C*V^2*f*toggle-rate form. Suggested: "α: probability that a node charges (0→1) in a cycle; a clock has α = 1. Some texts write ½·α'·C·V²·f with α' = toggles per cycle; same physics."
- F-PWR-2 (severity 2, non-canonical sequence). src/practice/verification.ts:666 (boss-upf) correct answer "Stop clocks → save retention → enable isolation → open the power switch". A commonly taught sequence is stop clock → assert isolation → save retention → power off — [ChipVerify: UPF retention strategies](https://chipverify.com/unified-power-format/upf-retention-strategies) (also summarized from Synopsys low-power glossary in search results). Both orders appear in industry (the hard constraints are: clocks stopped before save; save and isolation both complete before the switch opens), so the game's answer is defensible, but it is the less common textbook order and the distractor "Enable isolation → open the switch → save retention" makes isolation-first look wrong. Suggested: make the correct option "Stop clocks → enable isolation → save retention → open the power switch" and add to the explanation "save and isolation may be swapped as long as both precede switch-off".
- Clock gating via latch + AND ICG (vlsi.ts:152), isolation for off domains (vlsi.ts:153; practice/vlsi.ts:236; verification.ts:663), level shifters low→high (vlsi.ts:154; verification.ts:664), combined isolation+level-shift cell (verification.ts:665), retention with always-on latch (practice/vlsi.ts:238), SRAM retention voltage (practice/vlsi.ts:373), in-rush I = C·dV/dt with daisy-chained switches (verification.ts:668), DVFS ∝ V²f and race-to-idle / minimum-energy point (vlsi.ts:155-162; practice/vlsi.ts:240): all correct by standard textbook treatment (no contrary source found).
- UPF 4.0 (IEEE 1801-2024) adds AMS-related features (VCM, HDL tunneling), virtual supply nets and refinable macros — [SemiEngineering UPF 4.0](https://semiengineering.com/an-inside-look-at-upf-4-0). F-PWR-3 (severity 2, gap): the UPF content (practice/vlsi.ts:239) is version-agnostic; no UPF command is shown (e.g., create_power_domain, set_isolation, set_retention, set_level_shifter, add_power_state). One art block of real UPF would mirror the SDC block in vlsi.ts:47-52.
- IR drop: static I·R and dynamic L·di/dt are both taught (roles.ts:156-168; practice/vlsi.ts:230 notes "Dynamic (di/dt) drop adds on top of this static number"). Budget "typically 5-10%" (practice/vlsi.ts:230) is consistent with common practice; I found no authoritative single number (see Gaps). EM taught with temperature dependence and signal-net (AC) EM (silicon.ts:102). Correct.

### Inferences
- F-PWR-1 is the one item in this area most likely to cost a learner in an interview ("what is alpha for a clock?" is a classic question; the game never asks it).
- Adding "power-aware simulation corrupts state of off domains to X" is already implied (verification.ts:663); good.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source located for typical static vs dynamic IR-drop budgets (commonly quoted 2-5% static, ~10% dynamic); the game's "5-10%" is therefore unverified but plausible.
- Synopsys's own low-power glossary page could not be fetched (DNS failure), so the power-down order is supported by a secondary source only.

## 4. Design for Test (fault models, coverage, ATPG, compression, BIST, JTAG, test economics)

### Takeaway
DFT mechanics are correct (stuck-at activation/propagation, scan shift/capture, test-time formula, MATS 4N and March C- 10N, LFSR primitive polynomial period 2^n − 1, 16-state TAP, 5 × TMS=1 reset, Williams-Brown DL = 1 − Y^(1−FC), multi-site cost). The main gaps are the fault-coverage vs test-coverage distinction, modern fault models (cell-aware is only name-checked), compression architectures (EDT/DFTMAX/streaming scan), and the IEEE 1500/1687/1838 family; one LBIST claim overstates what safety standards require.

### Cited Findings
- Fault coverage counts all faults; test coverage excludes untestable faults (unused, tied, blocked, redundant), so the reported test coverage is higher; vendors categorize untestables differently — [Electronic Design: Debugging low test-coverage situations](https://www.electronicdesign.com/news/products/article/21760377/debugging-low-test-coverage-situations); [NYCU testing lab notes](https://tiger.iee.nycu.edu.tw/course/Testing2022Fall/notes/pdf/lab2_2022F.pdf).
  - F-DFT-1 (severity 3, gap/imprecision). Game defines only fault coverage = detected/total (src/practice/verification.ts:419-420; src/games/dft/levels.ts:125; glossary.ts:95). The DFT lab correctly notes "untestable faults also drag coverage down unless they are excluded from the figure" (src/games/dft/levels.ts:154) but never names that excluded figure "test coverage", which is the headline number in Tessent/TestMAX reports and a standard interview question. Suggested addition at dft/levels.ts:154: "Tools report both: fault coverage = DT/all faults, test coverage = DT/(all − untestable). Sign-off targets usually refer to test coverage."
- F-DFT-2 (severity 2, overstated claim). src/practice/verification.ts:473: "Why is logic BIST (LBIST) used in cars? — The chip can test itself in the field (at key-on), which safety standards require". ISO 26262 sets metrics (e.g., ASIL D latent-fault metric ≥ 90%) that can be met by LBIST, error-injection around safety mechanisms, or software test libraries, at reset/key-on/periodically; LBIST alone often cannot meet coverage within the diagnostic test time interval — [Siemens Tessent: How to use logic BIST for automotive functional safety](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/tessent/2020/06/17/how-to-use-logic-bist-for-automotive-functional-safety/); [Design&Reuse: LBIST, a technique for in-field safety](https://www.design-reuse.com/article/60751-lbist-a-technique-for-infield-safety/). Suggested: "...which helps meet ISO 26262 diagnostic/latent-fault coverage targets (LBIST is one common safety mechanism, often combined with in-system scan/hybrid LBIST)".
- Scan test time: practice uses P·(L+1)+L (src/practice/verification.ts:440-443; boss-dft :643) and the DFT lab uses (P+1)·L+P (src/games/dft/levels.ts:188). These are algebraically identical (PL+P+L); consistent. Correct.
- Williams-Brown DL = 1 − Y^(1−FC) (src/lessons/roles.ts:389; practice/verification.ts:425; silicon.ts:643; glossary.ts:162) — correct statement of the 1981 model (textbook; Bushnell & Agrawal). No contrary source.
- JTAG: 16-state TAP, TMS=1 for five TCKs reaches Test-Logic-Reset, IR path Run-Test/Idle →1→ Select-DR →1→ Select-IR →0→ Capture-IR →0→ Shift-IR (verification.ts:184; practice/verification.ts:459-464) — correct per IEEE 1149.1 state diagram (standard text; [Electronic Design 1149.1-2013 article](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/test-measurement/article/21800579/ieee-std-11491-this-isnt-your-fathers-jtag-anymore-part-1) for the standard's identity).
