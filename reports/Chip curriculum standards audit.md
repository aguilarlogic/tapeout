# TAPEOUT Teaches Well but Cites No Standards

Yes, industry standards and recognized curricula cover almost every TAPEOUT topic, and the game is largely accurate against them, but it names almost none of them and has drifted on several fast-moving ones. The curriculum anchors are ABET EAC 2026-2027, the NCEES FE Electrical & Computer spec (July 2020, due to be replaced July 2027) and IEEE/ACM CE2016 (still current; a successor exists only as an April 2025 mock-up). The domain anchors are IEEE 1800-2023 (SystemVerilog), IEEE 1800.2-2020 (UVM), IEEE 1801-2024 (UPF 4.0), POSIX.1-2024, C23, RISC-V unprivileged v20260120, ISO 26262:2018, IEEE 1241-2023 and PCIe 7.0. The seven audits found **no severity-5 error** (something that would badly hurt a learner), but **four severity-4 errors**: the C lab wraps signed overflow that C defines as undefined behaviour; the RISC-V ISA lab teaches 4- and 12-byte stack frames that violate the 16-byte psABI alignment; a verification drill marks a satisfiable constraint as unsatisfiable; and a security drill teaches CPA against the XOR output, which cannot distinguish a key from its complement. Roughly two dozen severity-3 items follow, plus many wording fixes. Staleness is concentrated where standards moved after about 2021: HBM is fixed at 1024 bits (HBM4 is 2048), refresh numbers are DDR4's, PCIe stops at Gen 6, AMBA still says master/slave, and backside power is called "next" when it ships. The largest coverage gaps are noise and stability theory (foundations), modern SystemVerilog/SVA/X-propagation (RTL), POCV/LVF and IEEE 1500/1687/1838 (implementation and DFT), FP8/MX formats and Rowhammer mitigations (architecture), ASIL determination, ISO/SAE 21434 and AEC-Q100 (safety/security), and linearity and jitter metrics (analog/RF).

This report follows the seven research notes. Items the researchers could not verify against a primary source are marked **[UNVERIFIED]** or **[PLAUSIBLE]** and collected in the final section. Severity runs 1 to 5, where 5 means it teaches something wrong that would hurt in an interview or on the job.

## Seven areas, forty-odd governing standards: current versions in October 2026

The standards picture divides cleanly into slow-moving curriculum frameworks and fast-moving domain standards, and the game's exposure differs between the two. For foundations, the ABET 2026-2027 criteria (changes approved by the Board of Delegates as of 24 October 2025) require probability and statistics, calculus, and for electrical programs "advanced mathematics, such as differential equations, linear algebra, complex variables, and discrete mathematics" ([ABET](https://www.abet.org/accreditation/accreditation-criteria/criteria-for-accrediting-engineering-programs-2026-2027/)). The most concrete topic checklist is the NCEES FE Electrical & Computer specification, effective with the July 2020 exams and still in force, with all FE modules being re-specified and new-spec exams "projected to go live in July 2027" ([NCEES spec](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/FE-Electrical-and-Computer-CBT-specs.pdf); [IEEE-USA Insight](https://insight.ieeeusa.org/?p=5124)). ACM lists CE2016 as the current computer-engineering curriculum, and a CE mock-up dated 2025-04-01 reproduces its 12 knowledge areas, 135 units and 420 core hours without announcing a successor ([ACM](https://www.acm.org/education/curricula-recommendations); [CSED CE Mockup](https://csed.acm.org/mockup-ce/)). The game should therefore cite CE2016 as current and label the 2020 FE spec "current until July 2027".

The tables below give, per area, the standard, its status as of October 2026, and how the game measures up. "Game names it" is almost always no; the notes' consistent conclusion is that the risk is omission, not staleness.

## The game's physics and arithmetic are sound; errors cluster in labs, drills and vocabulary

The headline pattern across all seven notes is that the core equations are right. The foundations researcher spot-checked dozens of results (RC time constants, Thevenin, V_T = kT/q, the 60 mV/decade rule, binary32 layout, Nyquist, the ΔΣ SQNR formulas) and found no error that would teach a wrong core formula. The implementation researcher reached the same verdict for setup/hold slack, multicycle -hold N-1 pairing, CRPR arithmetic, Elmore, logical effort, Williams-Brown, JTAG and scan test time. The architecture researcher found every AXI handshake, NoC and coherence claim correct, and the systems researcher found the RV32IM simulator spec-correct on every M-extension and shift edge case it probed, including divide-by-zero results taken from the ratified spec ([M extension](https://docs.riscv.org/reference/isa/v20260120/unpriv/m-st-ext.html)).

The errors that matter come from three recurring mechanisms. First, interactive labs contradict the lessons that precede them. The C lab silently wraps signed overflow although the lesson says it is undefined behaviour (`src/games/code/c.ts:743`; its test at `c.test.ts:15-16` enshrines the wrap), and the researcher judged this the highest-severity C finding because learners remember what they did hands-on. The security notes found the same pattern twice: the sec-dpa lab correctly attacks `SBOX[p ^ k]` while the drill at `practice/domains.ts:161-162` attacks the XOR, and the sec-defend lab models hiding correctly while the drill at `domains.ts:220` calls it masking. The foundations note found four more lesson-versus-practice inconsistencies (F2, F3, F4, F15 below), which suggests a consistency pass between `src/lessons/*.ts` and `src/practice/*.ts` would catch the class.

Second, simulators are simplified without disclosure. The RTL simulator, SV interpreter and SVA engine are all two-state, registers power up at 0, and `$isunknown` always returns 0, yet the simulator header claims this is "called out in the game's help text" and no learner-facing text says so (`src/sim/simulator.ts:3-6`). A missing-reset bug therefore looks like "stuck at 0" rather than X, and X-optimism, taught at `practice/verification.ts:86`, cannot be observed. The SVA engine also has two real bugs: `[->n]` and `[=n]` never report failure (`sva.ts:390-398`), and bounded `[=n]` drops trailing idle cycles. No current level uses these operators, so impact is limited to free-form experimentation.

Third, vocabulary and versions have drifted. Because the game rarely cites version numbers, almost nothing is wrong by version; what is stale is unlabelled generation-specific data (DDR4 refresh, 1024-bit HBM, 18×18 DSP blocks) and unlabelled flavour-specific syntax (GNU/PCRE regex, x86-style shift masking, MSVC debug-fill patterns). The impact is highest where an interviewer would spot it immediately. A candidate who says "HBM is 1024 bits" or "PCIe 6 is the newest" in 2026 sounds dated, and one who says "Q is 14 at 1e-12" or "Hamming first sidelobe is -53 dB" repeats a game phrase an interviewer will correct.

## Coverage gaps worth adding, ranked by interview and job value

The gaps below are topics the standards and curricula cover and the game does not teach. They are ordered by how often the researchers judged them to be interview-critical, and each is sized so it can become one lesson step, one practice item or one standards card.

**Cross-cutting, cheapest first.** A one-screen "standards you will hear on the job" card per act would close most naming gaps without touching gameplay (implementation note's recommendation), e.g. RTL: SystemVerilog is IEEE 1800-2023 with Verilog part of it since 2009, UVM is IEEE 1800.2-2020 with the 2020.3.1 library, plus PSS 3.0, UVM-MS 1.0, CDC/RDC 1.0, IP-XACT 1685-2022 and cocotb 2.x. For implementation: UPF 1801-2024, Liberty/LVF, SDC 2.1, SPEF, LEF/DEF, GDSII/OASIS, 1149.1/1500/1687/1838, STIL/STDF, IPC-2152/2141/6012/7352. Also state the data model and standard version each lab assumes (C23 vs C99-C17 behaviour, LP64, signed char, RARS-style ecalls).

**Foundations (priority order from the foundations note).** (1) Noise: kT/C, 4kTR, 1/f and noise bandwidth, all basic to ADC, PLL and analog interviews and listed under FE electrical properties. (2) Stability: gain margin, a Bode magnitude and phase sketch, and the Nyquist criterion at least qualitatively; the game teaches only phase margin. (3) IEEE 754 special values (±0, ±∞, NaN), subnormals, exponent bias and round-to-nearest-even. (4) MOSFET capacitances and the Miller effect, which underpin gate delay and logical effort. (5) Drift and diffusion currents with the Einstein relation. Also: complex-number mechanics (e^(jθ), phasor multiplication), Fourier series, inverse Laplace and final-value theorem, and one sentence on conventional current and the passive sign convention (a severity-2 gap; zero hits for "conventional current"). Three-phase power, motors, transformers and thyristors are FE content but low priority for chip roles; the note recommends omitting them while saying so (an inference, not a cited finding). The glossary also lacks KCL/KVL, Thevenin, phasor, Bode, Laplace, FFT, PID, thermal voltage, IEEE 754, BJT, current mirror and skin depth.

**Systems and software.** Tcl is quiz-only with no interpreter lab, yet DV postings ask for "Python, Perl, or TCL" ([Apple DV job](https://jobs.apple.com/en-ie/details/200446465/design-verification-engineer)); add `proc`, `dict`, `regexp`, `catch` and SDC-style `get_*`/filter. C undefined behaviour beyond overflow (strict aliasing, uninitialised reads, shifts), UBSan, `-Wall -Wextra`, `static_assert` on register offsets, memory barriers versus volatile, ISR shared-variable rules, and MISRA C:2025/CERT. RISC-V privileged content (CSRs, `mtvec`/`mepc`/`mcause`/`mret`, privilege modes, `fence`, A and C extensions, RV64), linker scripts and crt0 (`.bss` zeroing, `.data` copy), GDB/OpenOCD/JTAG. Git: `fetch` versus `pull`, remotes, `rebase -i`, submodules, LFS (the lab has no `rebase` though lessons teach it). Shell: quoting and word-splitting, `set -euo pipefail`, `find -print0 | xargs -0`, environment modules, csh/tcsh, GNU versus POSIX. Python: type hints, venv/pyproject, pytest, argparse, subprocess, cocotb. C++: rule of 0/3/5, `std::atomic`, `constexpr`. OS: an RTOS, interrupt latency and nesting, device tree. Glossary: no entries for git, regex, Make, Tcl, ABI or undefined behaviour.

## Conclusion

The audit's most useful finding is how the errors are shaped, not how many there are. Nearly all of the game's equations survived independent checking, and the four severity-4 items are all places where a hands-on lab or drill diverges from the game's own correct lesson or from a sibling lab (the C interpreter versus the UB lesson, the CPA drill versus the sec-dpa lab, the masking drill versus sec-defend, the 12-byte hints versus the 16-byte brief). That means a consistency pass between lessons, practice banks and labs is likely to retire a large share of the severity 3-4 list faster than any research would, and the coding agent should work the severity-4 and severity-3 tables first, then batch the severity 2 and 1 rows by file.

The second finding is that "up to standard" in this domain is a moving target, and the game's exposure is concentrated where vendors and consortia moved most since 2021: memory (HBM4, DDR5 refresh, Rowhammer PRAC), interconnect (PCIe 7, CXL 4, UCIe 3, AMBA terminology), low-precision AI formats, backside power, and the 2024-2026 verification standards (1800-2023, UPF 4.0, CDC/RDC 1.0). Because the game rarely cites version numbers, most of this can be repaired by labelling generations and adding standards cards rather than rewriting lessons. Two scheduled events will date the baseline again soon: the new NCEES FE specification in July 2027 and the ISO 26262 third edition around 2027-2028, so the fix list should cite versions in one place (the standards cards) where they are cheap to update.
